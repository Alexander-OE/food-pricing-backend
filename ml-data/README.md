# ml-data

**This is simulated (MOCK) data**, modelled on the format of the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) monthly food price reports.
It is generated by `scripts/generate-ml-data.ts` (`npm run data:generate`) with a fixed random seed, so the files never change unless the script does.
When `DATA_SOURCE=nbs` is implemented, the real NBS feed replaces these files.

Date range: **January 2020 to December 2024** (60 months). Every period is the 1st of the month (`YYYY-MM-01`).

| File | Rows | Columns | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `commodities.json` | 25 | `name, slug, category, unit, description, latestPrice` | The 25 tracked foods and their metadata. |
| `lagos_food_prices.csv` | 1,500 | `period, commodity_slug, price, source` | Monthly Lagos retail price in naira per unit. `source` is always `MOCK`. |
| `macro_indicators.csv` | 420 | `period, key, value` | 7 monthly indicators (see below) for the same 60 months. |
| `commodity_sensitivities.json` | 25 | `fx, diesel, seasonality, supply, inflation` (0 to 1 each) | How strongly each commodity reacts to each factor. Used by the forecast model. |

## Indicator keys
- `USD_NGN`: naira per US dollar
- `DIESEL_PRICE`, `PETROL_PRICE`: naira per litre
- `HEADLINE_INFLATION`, `FOOD_INFLATION`: annual percent
- `RAINFALL_INDEX`: 0 to 100, high in June to September
- `SUPPLY_INDEX`: 0 to 100, higher means more food supply (peaks after harvest, October to December)

## How the prices are built
1. Start from each commodity's December 2024 price (`latestPrice`).
2. Walk backwards using a general inflation path (about 16% a year in 2020 rising to 40% in 2024, with a visible jump in mid-2023).
3. Multiply by a yearly seasonal pattern (e.g. tomato is dearest March to July).
4. Add small random noise (2% for processed goods, up to 5% for fresh produce) and round to the nearest 10 naira.
